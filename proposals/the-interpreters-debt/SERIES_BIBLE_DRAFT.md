## 1. LOGLINE AND PREMISE
**One sentence:** A night-shift hotel interpreter overhears the man who ruined her father admit it in a language he thinks nobody in the room understands, and takes a job inside his family to prove that one mistranslated word stole her father's company.

**Why it works for this format:** a secret she can only use by breaking her own professional oath; an enemy's heir who slowly becomes an ally; a mentor who is secretly the traitor; and a mystery solved by **language**, which makes every scene a listening scene (close-ups, whispers, reactions — cheap and strong on screen).

**Central question (answered in Ep 30):** Can Nour prove that her father signed a *pledge* and not a *transfer* — and what will she do when the man who owes the debt is the one she has begun to trust?

**Title meaning (three debts):** (1) Nour's debt to her mentor Karam, who paid for her training, (2) the Vale family's debt to the Haddad family, (3) the debt Adrian finally chooses to pay.

**Age target:** all-ages / teen-safe. Light romance only (looks, hand-holding, one almost-kiss at most). No gore, no sexual content, no real people, no real brands. A parent's death happened years ago, off-screen, from illness.

## 2. BACKSTORY (the secret mechanism)
- **Eight years ago** Haddad Freight (Samir Haddad) and Vale Holdings (Edmund Vale) signed the **Harbour Accord** in a suite at the Halcyon Grand Hotel.
- The interpreter was young **Karam Younes**. Samir spoke little English and trusted him.
- **Arabic counterpart (what Samir signed):** Haddad Freight **pledges** its port licenses as security for a loan, **returned on repayment**. (Arabic word: *rahn* — pledge/collateral.)
- **English version (what Vale holds):** Haddad Freight **assigns** the port licenses **on default**. (English word: *assigns*.)
- Karam rendered *rahn* as *assigns*, paid by Edmund. The loan "defaulted" through a delayed payment Edmund arranged. Haddad Freight collapsed; Samir died two years later of a heart condition.
- Karam trained Nour and paid her brother Omar's tuition afterwards: guilt money, routed through a "Vale Heritage Fund".
- **The proof:** the **original Arabic counterpart** signed in Samir's **blue ink** (his special iron-gall blue ink, visible under a modern ink test), plus a hidden bank-box key inside Samir's **blue fountain pen**.

*(Note for the AI: have a native Arabic speaker confirm the exact Arabic words *rahn* / *tanāzul* before they appear on screen. Do not show Arabic text you have not verified.)*

## 8. MANAGER'S AUDIT OF THIS OUTLINE (honest)
**Strong:** hook rule met in all 30; midpoint twist (Ep 15) re-reads Eps 1–14; the clue ladder (whisper → framed page → box → pen → claim form → confession → voicemail → notes → logs → document → ink); the key word *pledge/assigns* is easy to dramatise; each episode can run on close-ups and reactions.

**Weak spots to fix in script phase (do not hide them):**
1. **Eps 24–25** are quieter. Add a small chase or a deadline (e.g. the hearing moves to tomorrow).
2. **Ep 19** risks a "misunderstanding" cliché; make Adrian's confession cost him something visible.
3. **Ep 23** needs a clear reason Edmund lets them walk out with the document (suggested: he is certain the NDA makes it useless — keep that logic explicit).
4. **Legal realism:** keep court procedure simple and generic. Have a legal consultant or well-sourced reference check Eps 17–18, 24, 27–29 before release. Mark anything uncertain as "fictional court".
5. **Romance** must stay light and all-ages.
6. **Nothing may copy** any existing drama (names, titles, scenes). The AI must run a similarity check against well-known stories and the example channels.
