## Season 1: The Last Little Fox — 12 episodes
**The emotional question:** What if the bravest thing an outcast can do is save the people who rejected him?  
**Season propulsion:** Each episode answers one small mystery, then reveals a deeper and more personal one. Ember's urgency drives action; Moss's concealed guilt creates conflict; Ash's divided loyalty makes the later confession earned. Luma's knowledge is useful but incomplete. Finale retains the **faulty signal lantern** as the origin of the fire, the failure to repair the alarm as the reason harm spread, and the forest rebuilding.

## Season 2: The Boy Who Forgot Tomorrow — 10 episodes
**The emotional question:** If you could see tomorrow but forgot the people you loved today, what future is worth protecting?  
**Season propulsion:** Build a personal contradiction each episode, then link it to the city's hidden memory shield. Do not turn the Clockkeeper into a cartoon villain: his fear of catastrophe explains his choices. Finale remains the shield's disabling, erased residents restored, and Noah losing future visions but gaining freedom.

| Ep | Story beat / more interesting version | Emotional turn | End hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 · A Note From Tomorrow | Noah wakes to a warning in his own handwriting, then sees his future self enter the forbidden tower anyway. | He fears his own choices. | Future-Noah turns to the window and looks directly at him. |
| 02 · The Girl No One Remembers | Mira remembers saving Noah, but her traces vanish as he tries to verify her. He believes her when her satchel contains a promise only he could have written. | Trust has to survive missing evidence. | Her name erases itself from his notebook as he watches. |
| 03 · The Clock That Runs Backward | The tower reverses seven minutes; small changes after each reset suggest the lost time is being edited, not repeated. | Noah sees what uncertainty does to friendship. | Midnight strikes thirteen. |
| 04 · Seven Minutes Missing | Recovered footage shows a street no map contains. Mira risks becoming forgotten again to lead Noah there. | She chooses proof over her safety. | The impossible street appears outside Noah's window. |
| 05 · The Man in the Yellow Coat | The Clockkeeper offers to restore Mira's memory records if Noah promises to stop digging. He knows a detailed future Noah has not seen. | Noah refuses a tempting personal rescue. | The Clockkeeper holds Noah's future drawing. |
| 06 · A Promise He Never Made | A hidden recording shows Noah once agreed to erase Mira as part of an emergency plan. Was the promise forced—or made to save her? | Noah begins doubting his own innocence. | Mira says he asked for her to be forgotten. |
| 07 · The City Stops at Midnight | Dr. Vale explains the shield's disaster-response reset, now malfunctioning. The team must choose whom to wake before the next reset. | The truth complicates the supposed villains. | Permanent memory erasure begins. |
| 08 · The Wrong Future | Alternative memories show the shield itself created the emergency it tried to prevent. The Clockkeeper traps Mira in the vault to protect everyone else. | Protecting people can become controlling them. | The vault seals with Mira inside. |
| 09 · One Memory to Save | Noah can stop the shield only by sacrificing tomorrow-visions. He must trust Mira in a future he can no longer predict. | He gives up certainty for love and agency. | The lever moves toward its final stop. |
| 10 · Tomorrow Begins Again | He disables the shield; erased residents return, the Clockkeeper takes responsibility, Mira is remembered, and Noah has no future visions. | He chooses an ordinary unknown tomorrow. | Complete resolution: he writes a note dated today. |

## Every episode's film-writing rules
- **First 0–5s:** startling *visual action*, not an explanatory title card.
- **Every 5–8s:** a new meaningful action, reaction, reveal, or motivated camera move. No static actor over a background.
- **Speech:** concise, character-specific lines. Genuine speech scenes require a tested audio-driven face/acting model; never use the rejected mouth-flap puppet.
- **Visuals:** the background is one integrated scene with its characters, shadows, volumetric lighting, props and camera. No giant dialogue cards or fake social UI.
- **Transitions:** visible physical continuity or a purposeful cut, never filler black frames or stretched stills.
- **Audio:** locked voices + world sound + readable dialogue in mix; do not pad the film to hit a duration.
- **Ending:** emotional turn followed by a visual question or satisfying finale, not an arbitrary cliffhanger.
- **Budget:** content preparation is $0; *generating 3–6 minutes of premium AI video every day for $0 is not currently established*. Stop on quota instead of charging or downgrading.
